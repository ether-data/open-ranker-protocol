# Code of Conduct

ORP is an open specification project that depends on multi-vendor participation. This Code of Conduct exists so that participation is sustainable and so that contributors of all backgrounds can engage constructively.

## Our Standards

We expect contributors to:

- **Engage constructively.** Technical critique is welcome and necessary. Personal critique is not. Critique the proposal, not the person.
- **Disclose conflicts of interest.** If you work for a vendor whose business is materially affected by a proposal you're discussing, say so. Disclosure does not exclude participation; it informs other contributors' weighting.
- **Use attribution discipline.** If you adopt an idea from elsewhere, cite it. ORP itself is built on the published work of many others; we maintain the same discipline.
- **Respect process.** RFC-style proposals exist so that substantive changes get substantive review. Skipping the process to push a change is not constructive participation.
- **Speak for yourself unless authorized to speak for your employer.** If you're representing an organization's position formally, say so. If you're sharing your own technical view, say that.
- **Treat questions seriously.** Newcomers to the spec are not failing; they're asking the questions that reveal what the spec needs to explain better.

## Unacceptable Behavior

The following are not acceptable in ORP project spaces:

- Harassment, discrimination, or personal attacks based on identity, background, or affiliation.
- Sustained disruption of discussion, including off-topic derailment or repeated bad-faith engagement.
- Publishing others' private information without explicit permission.
- Misrepresenting your affiliation or speaking on behalf of your employer without authorization.
- Use of the project for purposes that contradict its stated mission (e.g., proposals designed to fork the spec for narrow commercial gain at the cost of multi-vendor interoperability).

## Enforcement

Maintainers are responsible for enforcement and accountable to the community.

Concerns about conduct should be raised:

- In public if the issue is itself public-facing and constructive discussion is possible.
- Directly to a maintainer (by email) if the situation requires privacy.

Enforcement actions, in escalating order:
1. Private clarification or warning.
2. Public clarification or warning.
3. Temporary removal from project spaces.
4. Permanent removal from project spaces.

Maintainers commit to acting in good faith, considering context, and applying consistent standards.
